# Vision

## What Computation Quests is

A free, openly licensed collection of computer science, data science, computational thinking,
cybersecurity, and AI literacy materials for grades 6–12. It is presented as one map, a continent
divided into regions, each covering one subject area.

Each region gives teachers:

- lessons they can teach tomorrow
- unplugged activities for classrooms with limited devices
- browser-based activities that students use directly, with no accounts
- the background knowledge, misconceptions, and standards alignment a teacher needs

## The map

| Region | Location |
|---|---|
| Computational Thinking | The south; the starting point |
| Algorithms and Coding | East |
| Data Science | West |
| AI Literacy | The snowy north |
| Cybersecurity | An island off the west coast |
| Ethics and Impacts | The center, where every road meets |

Computational Thinking is the starting point because it underlies everything else. Ethics and
Impacts sits at the center, on every route between regions, because it belongs in every subject.

## Design principle: human-in-the-loop (unstated)

This is a design note for authors, not a message for the site or the map. Nothing in the branding
should say it outright.

As students move through Computation Quests, they should come to realize on their own that computing depends
on people at every step: people frame the problem, choose and label the data, write and test the
code, check the output, and decide what to do with it. Activities make this visible by needing the
student's judgment, for example:

- a model or program that only works once the student makes a decision it cannot make itself
- an AI output that looks right until the student checks it
- a dataset whose meaning depends on choices someone made when collecting it
- a security system that is only as strong as the person using it

Students should discover this through what they do, not read it in a headline. Ethics and Impacts
is where they reflect on it explicitly.

## Audiences

| Audience | What they need |
|---|---|
| CS teachers (MS/HS) | Standards-aligned lessons, fresh activities, depth |
| Teachers new to CS | A short content primer, clear lesson steps, common misconceptions |
| Cross-curricular teachers (math, science, social studies, ELA) | Short activities that bring computational thinking or data into their subject |
| Students | Engaging, self-explanatory interactive activities |

## Principles

1. **Free.** Nothing is paywalled.
2. **No student accounts or PII.** Activities run in the browser. Any sharing happens through
   links or class codes that contain no personal data. This avoids most COPPA and FERPA
   complexity.
3. **Consistent structure.** Every region follows the [area template](area-template.md).
4. **Classroom-tested.** Materials come from real classroom use and improve through feedback.
5. **Open.** CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 lets teachers adapt and share the materials.
