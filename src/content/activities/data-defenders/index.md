## What you'll do

Your school is full of information that could hurt someone if it got out: health records, lunch
money, grades, home addresses. You're the school's new security team. For each case file, you'll
figure out what kind of information is at risk and how sensitive it is, then spend a limited
budget on defenses to keep it **confidential**, meaning only the right people can see it. Then
another team tries to break in.

## How it works

1. **Open a case file.** Read what's collected, where it's kept, and who really needs it. Look for
   the risks that are already there, like a shared password or a laptop left open.
2. **Classify the data.** Tag each kind of information (personal, health, money, school record,
   location, login), and decide whether it's **public**, **private** or **highly sensitive**. That
   sets your budget of shields.
3. **Plan your defenses.** Spend your shields on defense cards, like two-step login, encryption or a
   lock and key. For each one, explain who it stops and how it keeps the data secret.
4. **Get attacked.** The red team plays threat cards, like a phishing email or a lost laptop. Block
   each one with a defense and explain how.
5. **Write it up.** If a threat gets through, write a breach report: what leaked, who could be
   harmed, and what you'd change. Then switch sides.

## Afterward

- Which case file had the most sensitive data? How did you decide?
- Which defense stopped the most threats? Which one did the least?
- Did you buy the Backup copy card? What does it really protect?
- Which defense would annoy the people who use the data most? Was it still worth it?
- Sometimes the best defense was to collect less. Where did that work?
