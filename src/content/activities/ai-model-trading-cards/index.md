## What you'll do

AI companies publish fact sheets called **model cards**. They say what a model learned from, what
it's good at, and where it fails. But a fact sheet only tells you what the maker wants to say. In
this activity you investigate six made-up AI tools, look at how each one did in real tests, and
find the weaknesses the maker left out. Then you turn what you found into trading cards.

## How it works

1. **Investigate.** Read a fact sheet for an AI tool, like a chatbot, an image maker or a face
   unlock. Mark each test in its test log right or wrong.
2. **Find the hidden weakness.** Look at the tests that went wrong. What do they have in common?
   Does the training data explain it?
3. **Rate the stats.** Give each tool 1 to 5 dots for **Reliability**, **Fairness**, **Privacy**
   and **Eco**, with a reason for each one.
4. **Make the card.** Draw an example of what the tool makes on the front. On the back, write what
   it was trained on, its weakness, what to watch out for, and patch notes: one fix for the next
   version.
5. **Play Right Tool for the Job.** For each mission, play the card that fits best, or play **No
   AI** if a person would do the job better. Back up your choice with evidence, and challenge
   choices you think are wrong.

## Afterward

- Which weakness surprised you most? Why do you think the maker didn't mention it?
- Can a tool be reliable but still unfair? Find an example in your cards.
- When was **No AI** the best play?
- If you made version 2 of a real AI tool you use, what would your patch notes say?
