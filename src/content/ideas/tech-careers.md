## What's really going on

Almost every industry now needs people who work with computers and data, from hospitals and sports
teams to video game studios and climate researchers. Tech jobs aren't all programming: some people
clean and analyze data, some build systems that keep data safe, and some lead teams and decide how
data gets used. Each job calls for different skills, and the schooling and pay vary a lot from one
to the next.

## Background

Computing and data careers span many job titles, and students often know only a few, such as
programmer or game designer. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook is a
reliable, regularly updated source for each job's median pay, typical education, and outlook.
Activities that put real job duties, salaries, and degree requirements in front of students help
them connect classroom skills to specific jobs and see more paths into the field.

## Misconceptions

- Every tech job is coding all day. Many roles focus on analysis, communication, design, security,
  or leadership.
- You need a Ph.D. to work in data science or AI. Many roles start with a bachelor's degree, and some
  start with a certificate or a two-year degree.
- Tech jobs are only at tech companies. Health care, government, sports, entertainment, and
  agriculture all hire people with computing and data skills.
