## Overview

Students learn to ask questions that data can answer. They collect, clean, and explore data,
build visualizations and simple models, and think critically about where data comes from and
who it affects.

## Big ideas (draft)

1. **Data is made by people for a purpose.** Every dataset reflects choices about what to
   collect, how, and from whom.
2. **Data must be organized and cleaned.** Structure (rows, columns, types) and cleaning come
   before analysis.
3. **Visualizations tell stories, and can mislead.** Choosing and reading charts critically.
4. **Patterns and models help us predict, with uncertainty.** From trends to simple models to
   the ideas behind machine learning.
5. **Data has consequences.** Privacy, bias, and fairness in how data is collected and used.

These follow the data investigation cycle: **ask a question → collect or consider data → analyze
→ interpret and communicate**.

## Learning progression (draft)

| | Middle school (6–8) | High school (9–12) |
|---|---|---|
| Questions | Ask statistical questions about familiar topics | Form questions about complex systems |
| Collect and clean | Collect class data on paper data cards; spot and fix messy entries | Sort, filter, and reshape a large printed deck of data cards |
| Visualize | Draw a fitting chart by hand; read charts critically | Build human and hand-drawn charts; find and fix misleading ones |
| Model | Describe trends and make predictions | Build and test models by hand, such as a decision tree from data cards |
| Impacts | Personal data and privacy | Bias, fairness, and data in decision-making |

## Activity ideas

Unplugged ideas, one per place on the map, to write up as activities. Many use **data cards**: one
index card per person or thing, with the same few fields on each (for students, ask only about
harmless things, such as favorite fruit, pets, or travel time to school).

| Place | Idea | What students do |
|---|---|---|
| Asking Questions | **Question Sort** | Students sort question cards into statistical and non-statistical, then sharpen vague questions into ones data could answer. |
| Sampling | **Bean Bags** | Students estimate the mix of colors in a bag of beans from small samples, then see how a biased scoop gives the wrong answer. |
| Data Collection | **Data Cards** | The class designs a short survey, fills out one data card each, and discusses what the cards leave out. |
| Outliers | **The Odd Card** | A planted outlier in the deck: groups decide whether it is a typo to fix or a real case to explain. |
| Data Cleaning | **Messy Deck** | Groups clean a printed deck with typos, blanks, and duplicates, and record each fix they made. |
| Organizing Data | **Paper Table** | Students lay the cards out as rows and columns on the floor and decide each column's data type. |
| Exploring Data | **Sort and Count** | Groups sort and tally the deck to find patterns, clusters, and relationships. |
| Visualization | **Human Charts** | Students stand to form a bar chart or scatterplot on floor axes, then redraw it by hand; a gallery walk of misleading charts follows. |
| Modeling | **Decision Tree** | Groups build a paper decision tree that sorts the cards, then test it on cards it has not seen. |
| Prediction | **Forecast Cards** | Students make predictions from their model with a confidence level, then check them against held-back cards. |
| Interpreting Results | **Claim, Evidence, Reasoning** | Groups write a claim on poster paper with the evidence from their cards, and classmates challenge it. |
| Privacy and Fairness | **What the Cards Reveal** | Students work out what can be guessed about a person from a few "harmless" fields, and who their model treats unfairly. |
