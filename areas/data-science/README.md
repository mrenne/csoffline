## Overview

Students learn to ask questions that data can answer. They collect, clean, and explore data,
build visualizations and simple models, and think critically about where data comes from and
who it affects.

## Big ideas (draft)

1. **Data is made by people for a purpose.** Every dataset reflects choices about what to
   collect, how, and from whom.
2. **Data must be organized and cleaned.** Structure (rows, columns, types) and cleaning come
   before analysis.
3. **Visualizations tell stories, and can mislead.** Choosing and reading charts critically.
4. **Patterns and models help us predict, with uncertainty.** From trends to simple models to
   the ideas behind machine learning.
5. **Data has consequences.** Privacy, bias, and fairness in how data is collected and used.

These follow the data investigation cycle: **ask a question → collect or consider data → analyze
→ interpret and communicate**.

## Learning progression (draft)

| | Middle school (6–8) | High school (9–12) |
|---|---|---|
| Questions | Ask statistical questions about familiar topics | Form questions about complex systems |
| Collect & clean | Collect class data; spot and fix messy entries | Work with large real-world datasets; transform and reshape them |
| Visualize | Choose a fitting chart; read charts critically | Build interactive visualizations; find misleading ones |
| Model | Describe trends and make predictions | Build and evaluate computational models |
| Impacts | Personal data and privacy | Bias, fairness, and data in decision-making |
